the problem

Self-learning is easy to start and easy to drop

People start free courses all the time, and most quietly stop. Nobody notices, because nobody is checking. The apps that do track you want an account, keep your data in their cloud, and still can't show anyone that you really did the work.

inPace adds a person you trust: a friend, parent or mentor. They check your progress by swapping files, they don't need to know the subject, and nothing goes through a server. Your history is signed, so it can't be quietly rewritten.

How the checking works

two people, two apps, no server in between
  1. 1ConnectSend your friend code on Telegram or WhatsApp. Your checker pastes it in.
  2. 2LearnLessons, then tasks: upload your work, explain it, take a quiz.
  3. 3Save a milestoneSigned with your key and linked to the one before it.
  4. 4Send the fileYour progress file goes by WhatsApp, email or USB.
  5. 5They checkTheir inPace verifies the signatures and shows what changed.
  6. 6ApproveThey send back a small reply file. Your milestone is approved.
  • What it provesThe course steps were followed in order, the work you added exists, and your history wasn't changed after your checker saw it.
  • What it doesn't proveThat every minute was real study. No app can, and inPace says so plainly. Verified exams and the checker's private questions make the proof stronger.

Under the hood

a small website that only lives on your computer
  • A local server in its own windowOpening inPace starts a tiny server only your computer can reach (127.0.0.1) and shows it in an app window: the system web view on Windows and Mac, GTK and WebKit on Linux, then Chrome or Edge app mode, then a browser tab.
  • One data folderEverything lives in Documents/inPace: the signing key, an SQLite history, courses, your uploaded work (named by SHA-256 fingerprint) and backups.
  • History you can't rewriteThe history is append-only: nothing past is edited or deleted. Each milestone is signed with an Ed25519 key that never leaves your computer and linked to the one before it, so any change breaks the chain.
  • Courses are plain textA course is a zip of YAML and Markdown. Questions and answers live in separate files, so answers can be left out when sharing, and quizzes mix one right answer with wrong ones in a new order each time.
  • Big courses aren't copiedA 60 GB folder of videos becomes a course in seconds: inPace only remembers where the folder is, and finds it again if it was moved.
  • Releases that check themselvesA version tag makes GitHub test everything on Windows, Mac and Linux, build the apps, check each one starts, and publish them with a SHA256SUMS file the updater verifies.

What it does

screenshots from the app
1

Add a course five ways

  • With any AI chat: inPace writes the prompt from a topic, a book, a playlist, a course you're taking, a project, your notes or an exam.
  • From a folder of videos you already have, or a list of YouTube links.
  • From a course file someone shared, or by hand in the course builder.
2

Learn, step by step

  • A trail of lessons, then tasks: upload your work, explain it in your own words, take a quiz. The next module unlocks when you finish.
  • A video player that remembers the second you stopped, a PDF reader that remembers the page, and code shown in color with a Copy button.
  • Daily review brings old questions back over days and weeks.
3

Find your way

Search anything with Ctrl+K: lessons, courses, tasks, people and pages. Continue where you left off, open the course outline on any lesson, and move with Alt and the arrow keys.

4

Stay on track, together

  • Weekly goals, streaks and a finish date. Planned breaks don't count against you.
  • Connect with a checker in People. Race friends, where only approved milestones count.
  • Verified exams, and a learning record anyone can verify.
5

Make your own course

The course builder: add modules, lessons and tasks, move them around, write quizzes, and undo any change. Share it with answers left out for learners, or included for checkers.

6

Make it yours

Seven color profiles (Sunny, Ocean, Mint, Rose, Grape, Nile and Ink) in light and dark, English and Arabic right to left, and Tempo the turtle cheering you on.
