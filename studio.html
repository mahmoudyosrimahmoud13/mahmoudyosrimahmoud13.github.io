the problem

Small shops can't afford a photo studio

Small shops shoot products on a phone, on whatever table is free. The backgrounds are messy, the light is yellow at night, and every photo has a different size and margin, so the catalog looks cheap. Online background removers cost money per photo and upload the shop's photos to someone else's server.

Studio does the whole job on the shop's own laptop, with no GPU, no account and no upload, and makes every photo match.

The pipeline

a slow step that runs once, and a fast step that runs on every change
  1. 1load()Opens the photo the right way up, reads iPhone HEIC, shrinks huge photos.
  2. 2mask()The AI gives every pixel a 0 to 1 product score. About 1.2 s, done once and kept.
  3. 3render()Everything else, about 0.4 s, on every change you make.

The editor keeps each photo's mask, so moving a slider never waits for the AI again. Inside render(), the steps always run in this order:

  1. 1Touch-upsYour Keep and Remove paint is merged into the mask.
  2. 2GeometryCrop, rotate, mirror and straighten move the photo and mask together.
  3. 3Clean edgesStray bits dropped, edge pixels decontaminated, so no halo.
  4. 4Light and colorWhite balance from neutral pixels only, gentle stretch, then your sliders.
  5. 5PlacementCut to the product, scaled and placed with the same margin every time.
  6. 6FinishSoft shadow, background, logo, then WebP, JPG or PNG.
  • Colors that stay trueThe white balance is read only from pixels that look neutral (walls, whites, grays), never from the product, at half strength and at most 8% per color. A maroon dress stays maroon. If there is too little neutral area, nothing changes.
  • Warnings measured, not guessedSmall: short side under 1000 px. Dark: the whole photo's average, so a black shirt on a light table is fine. Blurry: measured along the product's edges. Cut off: the product touches a side.
  • No haloEdge pixels are decontaminated: the old background's color is pulled out of them before the new background goes in.
  • Safe by defaultModels are checked by SHA-256 before use, huge "decompression bomb" files are refused, results are deleted after 24 hours, and the page listens only on your own computer.

Use it from Python
from studio.pipeline import load, mask, render, Options, Edits

img = load(open("dress.jpg", "rb").read())
m = mask(img, "best")                      # slow, once
r = render(img, m, Options(background="#FFFFFF", shape="portrait"),
           Edits(crop=(0.1, 0.05, 0.8, 0.9), warmth=0.2))   # fast, as often as you like
open("dress-clean.webp", "wb").write(r.data)
print(r.warnings)                          # for example ['small', 'blurry']

Measured against known truth

real photos, with problems I added on purpose, so the right answer is known

I took 8 real photos from open-source projects (sushi, a bike, a desk lamp, a cassette in a hand, an outfit, a dress, plants and grocery bags) and made copies with one known problem each: warm bulb light, green tube light, too dark, blurry, too small, and cut off. Because I added the problem myself, the original is the truth, and every result can be scored by code.

Color error is ΔE in Lab color, the standard way to measure how different two colors look. About 2 to 3 is hard to see.

  • What the numbers taught meThe automatic color fix is gentle on purpose, so it never ruins a product's real color. That also means it can't undo strong colored light on its own. For that, one tap on a white or gray spot does the job.
  • Where Fast falls shortFast and Best agree on 99% of pixels for simple photos, but only 46% on the busy plants photo. That's why Studio says: Fast for plain products, Best for busy photos.

Every feature

all screenshots from the real app, running on the test photos
1

One batch, one look

Pick the look once on the left, then drop up to 50 photos (iPhone HEIC works). Each one comes back cut out, centered, with the same margin, background and shadow.

  • Speed: Best edges or Fast.
  • Background: white, soft gray, any color, or see-through.
  • Shape: square, tall 4:5 for clothes, or the photo's own shape.
  • Position: centered, or standing on the floor.
  • Download all as one ZIP.
2

Warnings before you post

Each photo gets plain notes on what to fix next time you shoot: too small, too dark, blurry, cut off, or the product wasn't found clearly.

3

Compare

Before and after, side by side, for any photo.

4

Fix colors with one tap

Open a photo, go to Fix colors and tap something that should be white or gray. The colors are corrected from that spot. There's also a green and pink slider, and the automatic fix can be turned off per photo.

5

Touch up what the AI missed

The red overlay shows what will be removed. Paint Remove over leftovers, like the hand holding this cassette, or Keep over parts the AI dropped.

6

Crop, rotate and sliders

Crop free, 1:1 or 4:5. Rotate, mirror and straighten. Brightness, contrast, color, warmth, sharpness, and an own product size per photo. The result updates live.

7

File names and your logo

Type a product code like NM-102 and the photos become NM-102-1, NM-102-2, in the order shown. Add a logo in a corner or the middle, with its own size and strength.

8

Arabic and dark

The whole page works in Arabic, right to left, and in dark mode.

9

No page needed

./run.sh clean cleans a whole folder from the terminal, with the same warnings. Options for background, shape, file type and no enhancing.
